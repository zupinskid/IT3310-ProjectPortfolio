# User Persona and Product Vision: RCHN Patient Intake and Scheduling System

**Consultant:** David Zupinski
**Role:** Associate Consultant, Product Manager, NovaTech Consulting
**Engagement:** Riverside Community Health Network (RCHN)
**Date:** September 22, 2026

---

## User Persona

**Name:** Maya
**Role:** Intake Coordinator
**Years in Role:** 3 – 7 years
**Tech Comfort Level:** Medium, comfortable with common tools

Maya is a fictional representation of an RCHN intake coordinator. Her experience range and technology comfort are illustrative assumptions; her needs and frustrations are grounded in the scenario research.

### Primary Work Goal

Register patients accurately and complete appointment scheduling efficiently, with confidence that each booking has been recorded.

### Main Frustration with the Current System

A single registration requires too many screens, and unclear save results make it difficult to know whether an appointment was actually booked. These problems interrupt her workflow and create avoidable checking and rework. The paper backups used by front desk staff at two clinics show the broader loss of trust in the current system.

### What Would Make Their Work Better

A shorter, connected intake and scheduling workflow that preserves entered information, shows current appointment availability, and provides a clear confirmation after a successful save. It should explain failed saves and help prevent duplicate bookings so staff can resolve problems without relying on separate paper records.

---

## Product Vision Statement

For RCHN intake coordinators across the six clinics who need to register patients efficiently and know that appointments are recorded correctly, a unified patient intake and scheduling system will reduce unnecessary screen navigation and scheduling rework, prevent double bookings, and give staff confidence in appointment records.

### Rationale

Intake coordinators directly experience the registration workflow that the replacement system must improve. The finding that registration requires too many screens supports the efficiency need. Reports of uncertain appointment saves and double bookings support dependable scheduling feedback, while paper backups at two clinics show why rebuilding trust matters. Medical assistants’ repeated entry of patient information also supports a connected workflow that preserves data.

For RCHN, the intended value is less avoidable administrative work, fewer scheduling conflicts, and more dependable information across clinics. Shared scheduling visibility and consistent reports would also benefit managers and leadership. These are intended outcomes, not measured results. The team should establish current registration times, double-booking frequency, and paper-backup use, then compare them after rollout and gather staff feedback. These outcomes can guide later prioritization decisions.

### Scenario Evidence

Based on the fictional research findings in the [Week 3 assignment](https://jaguars.acm.org/courses/it-3310/week3/assignment.html). No independent interviews or measured improvements are claimed.
